The short version: pick the type of machine your business actually needs first. Then compare total monthly cost, not the price of the machine. Then check the contract length before you sign. Most small businesses overpay because they did those three things in the wrong order, or skipped the last one.

The three types of card machine

TypeWhat it isBest for
Pocket readerA small reader that pairs with your phone or tablet by Bluetooth. Some phones can now take payments with no reader at all ("tap to pay").Low takings, mobile trades, market stalls, occasional card payments.
Portable terminalA standalone machine with its own screen, Wi-Fi and usually a 4G SIM, so it works anywhere. Most print receipts.Restaurants and bars (pay at table), takeaways with delivery, busy shops.
Countertop terminalA machine fixed at the till, usually wired to the internet and often linked to your till system (EPOS).Fixed tills: retail, phone repair shops, hotel reception.

Features that actually matter

  • Connection. Wi-Fi is fine behind a till. Anything that leaves the building needs 4G. A built-in SIM may add a monthly charge.
  • Battery. A portable machine should last a full shift. Ask how many transactions a charge covers.
  • Receipts. A built-in printer matters for restaurants and anyone whose customers expect paper. Elsewhere, email or text receipts are fine.
  • Tipping. Tips prompted on the machine are charged at the card rate too, so they count towards your takings.
  • Offline mode. Lets you take payments when the signal drops, then sends them later. Useful at events, with some risk if a card is declined afterwards.
  • Till (EPOS) link. Sends the amount from your till to the card machine, so staff can't key in the wrong figure. Check it works with your till before you sign.

Everything else, from colour screens to loyalty apps, is nice to have. Don't pay a higher rate for it.

Buy, rent or pay-as-you-go

There are three ways to pay for a machine:

  • Buy a reader and pay a flat rate per sale.
  • Rent a terminal on a contract, with lower rates.
  • Buy a terminal outright and take a contract rate without paying rental.

Which is cheapest depends almost entirely on how much you take on card. Our rent vs buy guide shows 18-month totals at three sizes of business. Our cheapest card machine guide shows where the break-even point usually falls.

Payout speed: when your money arrives

"Settlement" means the money from card sales landing in your bank account. Next working day is common. Some providers offer same-day or even instant payouts, sometimes for an extra fee. If cash flow is tight, ask:

  • when money from a Friday night arrives;
  • whether faster payouts cost extra;
  • whether payouts go to your own bank or into an account held by the provider.

What to check before you sign

  1. Every fee, in writing. Debit rate, credit rate, business card rate, authorisation fee, rental, PCI and minimum monthly charge, with VAT shown. Our fees guide lists every charge to ask about.
  2. The term. For most small businesses, a new card machine lease or rental from the main providers can't have a first term longer than 18 months. After that it must end or run month to month. See how to get out of a card machine contract.
  3. Who the lease is with. Sometimes the machine is leased from a separate finance company, and that lease has its own terms.
  4. Whether rates can rise. Some contracts let the provider change your rates with notice. Ask what notice you get.
  5. The summary box. The main UK providers must give you a box summarising your key prices. If you can't find it, ask for it.
