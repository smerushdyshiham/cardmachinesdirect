The short version: hospitality needs more from a card machine than most trades: pay at table, tips, split bills and tabs. It also has a cost problem most trades don't: lots of small sales. When your average sale is £4, a 3p fee per transaction adds 0.75% to every payment. Check that fee first.

What hospitality needs from a card machine

  • Pay at table. Portable machines with Wi-Fi or 4G, so the bill comes to the customer.
  • Tipping on the machine. Tip prompts raise tips. They're charged at your card rate too, so they count as takings.
  • Split bills. Several cards on one bill, without staff doing sums.
  • Pre-authorisation. Holding an amount on a card for a bar tab or a hotel deposit, then taking the final amount later.
  • A link to your till (EPOS). The bill goes straight from the till to the machine, so nobody keys in the wrong amount.
  • More than one machine. A busy restaurant may need three or four. Rental on each adds up.

Takeaways: speed, deliveries and small orders

Takeaways need fast contactless at the counter and a machine with 4G for deliveries. The cost to watch is the per-transaction fee. A takeaway with 600 orders a month at 3p each pays £21.60 a month in authorisation fees with VAT. On a deal with no per-transaction fee, that line is £0.

Restaurants, cafés and bars

  • Cafés have the smallest average sale in hospitality, so per-transaction fees hurt most. A deal with a slightly higher percentage and no authorisation fee can be cheaper.
  • Restaurants have larger bills, so the percentage matters more than the fixed fees. Pay at table and tipping are essentials.
  • Bars and pubs need speed on busy nights and pre-authorisation for tabs. Make sure the machine can work offline if the signal drops.

Hotels: pre-authorisation and deposits

Hotels hold card amounts at check-in and take the final bill at check-out. They also see more credit cards, more business cards and more foreign cards than most trades, and those cost more to accept. Check these rates as closely as the debit rate. Our interchange guide explains why business and foreign cards cost more.

Why small sales make per-transaction fees matter

An authorisation fee is a flat charge each time a payment is approved. It's the same on a £4 coffee as on a £60 meal, so it hits small sales hardest:

Contactless limits after March 2026

The UK contactless limit had been £100 since October 2021. From 19 March 2026, the Financial Conduct Authority removed the regulatory limit and let card issuers set their own.1 So far, the major UK banks have kept £100. Higher limits will also need card machines and scheme rules to catch up.

For now, plan on £100. Above that, customers insert their card or use a phone wallet, which has no fixed limit.
