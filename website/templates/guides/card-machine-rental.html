The short version: at low card takings, a pay-as-you-go reader with no contract is usually cheapest. As takings grow, a contract with lower rates wins, and the monthly rental becomes a small part of the bill. Over 18 months the difference can be hundreds of pounds either way, so do the sum with your own numbers.

Three ways to get a card machine

  • No contract (pay-as-you-go). Buy a reader, usually £19 to £79 plus VAT, and pay a flat rate on every sale, typically 1.69% to 1.75%. No monthly fee, and you can stop whenever you like.
  • Rent on a contract. Pay a monthly rental, typically £10 to £25 plus VAT, in return for lower debit and credit rates. Repairs and replacements are usually included.
  • Buy a terminal and take a contract rate. Pay up front for the machine, then contract rates with no rental. Check that the terminal will work with another provider if you move. Many are locked to the company that sold them.

What each option costs over 18 months

We've used 18 months because that's the longest first term most new card machine contracts can now have. The example rates are:

  • Pay-as-you-go: 1.69% on every card, plus a £29 reader.
  • Rent: 1.1% debit, 1.6% credit, 2p per transaction, £18 a month rental.
  • Buy: the same contract rates with no rental, plus a £179 terminal.

VAT is added to the machine, rental and per-transaction fees. Every example assumes a £20 average sale and nine in ten payments on debit.

When renting makes sense

  • Your card takings are steady and above a few thousand pounds a month.
  • You want repairs and replacements covered without buying a new machine.
  • You need more than one machine, for example for pay at table, and don't want to buy them all up front.
  • The term is 18 months or less, and it rolls monthly after that.

When buying or no contract makes sense

  • Your takings are low, seasonal or unpredictable.
  • You're just starting out and don't know your volumes yet.
  • You want to be able to walk away at any time.
  • You're buying a terminal that works with more than one provider, so you can switch without replacing it.

Short-term hire for events and seasonal trade

If you only need card payments for a festival, a wedding fair or a summer season, you have two options:

  • A pay-as-you-go reader is usually cheaper than short-term hire. It costs about the same as a few weeks' rental, and you keep it for next time.
  • Short-term hire makes more sense if you need several terminals at once, or a guaranteed 4G connection at a busy event.

Before any event, check two things: that the reader works offline, and that its battery lasts a full day.

Lease traps to avoid

  • Long, non-cancellable leases. Before 2023, some businesses signed 36- and 48-month machine leases they couldn't leave. Those can still be running. See how to get out of a card machine contract.
  • Paying many times the machine's value. £25 a month for four years is £1,200 plus VAT for a machine that might cost £150 to buy.
  • A separate finance agreement. If the lease is with a finance company rather than your provider, cancelling your payment contract may not end the lease.
  • Automatic renewal. Check what happens at the end of the term, and diary the notice date.
