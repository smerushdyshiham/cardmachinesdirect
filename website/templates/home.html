{# ------------------------------------------------------------- PROBLEM #}

The rate you were sold isn't the rate you pay.

Card machine salespeople quote one number: the debit card rate. Your statement charges you for a lot more than that. Here's a typical month for a café taking £8,000 on card.

  1. 1

    The headline rate only covers debit cards

    Credit cards, business cards and overseas cards are charged more, often much more. They're rarely mentioned when you sign.

  2. 2

    A fee every time someone taps

    An authorisation fee is a flat charge per transaction, whatever the amount. At 4.5p on a £12 coffee and cake, it adds almost 0.4% on top of your rate.

  3. 3

    Rental that outlives the machine

    Leases signed before January 2023 could run for four years with no way out. Many are still running.

  4. 4

    Paying to be told you're not compliant

    A monthly "PCI" security fee, plus a penalty if you haven't filled in a yearly form nobody told you about.

  5. 5

    VAT on the fixed fees

    Percentage fees are VAT-free. Rental, authorisation and PCI fees aren't, so add 20% to those.

"The card machine is free."

It's paid for in your rate or your rental. Nothing about it is free. You're just not shown the price.

"Your rate is fixed."

Intro rates often rise when the first term ends, and a rising rate is easy to miss on a long statement. What fair looks like.

"You're stuck in your contract."

Since January 2023, new card machine leases can't lock you in for more than 18 months. How to get out.

{# ----------------------------------------------------------------- FAQ #}

Straight answers.

Is the saving above a quote?

No. It's an estimate built from your monthly takings, your average sale and a typical 90/10 split between debit and credit cards. Your real card mix changes the answer, which is why we ask for a statement before we quote.

How is the estimate worked out?

For each provider: debit takings times the debit rate, plus credit takings times the credit rate, plus the number of transactions times any per-transaction fee, plus the monthly fee. VAT is added to the fixed fees where a provider's price leaves it out. We do the same sum for our cheapest option.

Why don't you say which provider it is?

Because the answer changes with your numbers. The cheapest option for a takeaway doing £4,000 a month isn't the cheapest for a hotel doing £60,000. We match you to the cheapest available option for your business and put every fee in writing, so you can judge it on price alone.

Will I need a new card machine?

Usually, yes. Each provider supplies its own machines. Your quote shows what the machine costs, if anything, so it's in the comparison.

I'm in a contract. Is there any point?

Often, yes. Check when your term ends and what leaving early would cost. Sometimes the saving covers the exit fee within months. Our guide to leaving a contract walks through it.
